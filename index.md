     - No more foreign subawards/consortia in new applications after September 30, 2025.
     - Instead, use a "multi-component" application where foreign partners are treated as independent "International Projects."
     - Existing awards with foreign subs can continue, but renewals must transition.

#### 2. **New Application and Award Structure**
   - **Activity Codes for International Collaborations**:
     - **PF5 (Collaborative International Research Project Grant)**: For U.S. prime awards (grants) with linked foreign components. Must include at least one foreign subproject.
     - **UF5**: Similar for cooperative agreements.
     - **RF2/UL2 (Linked International Research Project)**: Independent awards issued directly to foreign organizations after the prime award is split.
     - Parent Announcement: [PA-26-002](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/pa-files/PA-26-002.html) (released January 20, 2026) supports PF5 for clinical trials optional. First awards expected in April 2027.
   - **How It Works**:
     - Submit a single multi-component application to a NOFO supporting PF5/UF5.
     - List each foreign collaborator as a separate "International Project" component.
     - If funded, NIH splits it: U.S. gets PF5/UF5; foreign gets RF2/UL2 as direct awards.
     - Foreign organizations must register in [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov), [Grants.gov](https://grants.gov), and [eRA Commons](https://era.nih.gov) (a process that can take 4-6 weeks).
   - **Benefits**: Direct foreign awards improve transparency and allow foreign PIs to build independent track records. It also facilitates multi-country studies (e.g., Uganda-U.S. partnerships on kidney transplants or mental health).
   - **Examples of Programs**:
     - **Fogarty Global Health Training Program**: Builds research capacity in LMICs.
     - **NIAID Global Infectious Disease Research**: Supports collaborations on HIV, malaria, etc.
     - **NIMH Global Mental Health Research**: Funds international mental health studies, aligning with your intern's work on depression mapping.

#### 3. **Strategies for Successful International NIH Collaborations**
Building on the NIH grant strategies we discussed earlier, here are tailored tips for international contexts:
   - **Start with Strong U.S. Partnerships**: As a Ugandan researcher, partner with U.S. institutions (e.g., Johns Hopkins) early. They can serve as the prime applicant, boosting scores on "investigators" and "environment." Leverage networks like the African Academy of Sciences or U.S.-Africa research forums.
   - **Align with NIH Priorities**: Focus on high-funding areas (e.g., Alzheimer's, HIV, global health equity). Use tools like NIH RePORTER to find similar funded projects and contact PIs for collaboration.
   - **Address Review Criteria Explicitly**:
     - **Importance**: Emphasize global impact (e.g., how Ugandan data on APOL1 genes informs U.S. health disparities).
     - **Rigor**: Include pilot data (e.g., your depression mapping app as a prototype for kidney disease).
     - **Expertise/Resources**: Highlight diverse teams (e.g., Ugandan clinicians + U.S. epidemiologists). For foreign components, justify why the international site is essential (e.g., unique populations in Uganda).
   - **Navigate Registration and Compliance**: Foreign entities need U.S.-style systems (e.g., IRB equivalents). Budget for international travel, data sharing (via platforms like dbGaP), and security reviews.
   - **Funding Mechanisms for Internationals**:
     - Direct Foreign Grants: Rare but possible for exceptional proposals (e.g., R01 equivalents).
     - Training/Capacity Building: D43 or U2R for LMIC institutions.
     - Multi-Site: U01 for cooperative studies (e.g., cross-Africa mental health research).
   - **Overcome Challenges**:
     - **Security Scrutiny**: NIH reviews foreign involvement for risks; disclose all affiliations transparently.
     - **Budgeting**: Foreign components get ~8-10% indirect costs (vs. U.S. 40-50%); plan accordingly.
     - **Resubmissions**: Use reviewer feedback—international proposals often need 1-2 iterations.
   - **Timeline Tips**: For PF5, aim for cycles starting February 2026. Contact NIH program officers (e.g., via [NIH Contacts](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/stafflist.htm)) for pre-submission advice.
   - **Success Examples**: Programs like the Human Heredity and Health in Africa (H3Africa) consortium have funded Ugandan-led genetics research. Recent PF5 awards support U.S.-Africa collaborations on infectious diseases.
